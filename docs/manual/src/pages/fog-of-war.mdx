# Fog of war

You do not see the whole board. You see what your troops can sense — and nothing else. This page covers exactly where your vision ends, what enemies see, and what spectators get.

## Your sensor horizon

Each of your troop stacks senses its surroundings out to a **radius of 4 cells** (measured in any direction, counting diagonals — a square around the stack). Your total vision is the **union** of these radii across all of your stacks:

- More stacks in an area means a wider combined horizon.
- A single deep raiding stack carries its own bubble of vision with it.
- Everything inside your horizon is drawn normally: terrain, cities, enemy troops and pipes.

## Outside the horizon

Ground beyond 4 cells of every stack you own is hidden:

- Enemy troops and pipes outside your horizon are invisible — attacks can come from the dark.
- You get no memory of what you saw: ground your stacks abandon **goes dark again**. There is no radar trail, no last-known-position ghosting; if you saw a column pass through a cell and marched away, that cell is blank until one of your stacks sees it again.
- Terrain itself is only revealed by vision too — the map is as unknown to you as to your opponent at tick zero.

Both players labor under exactly the same fog: there is no peeking, for anyone.

## Playing in the fog

- Assume unseen troops: a sudden quiet on the front often means movement somewhere you cannot see.
- Keep a cheap stack on overwatch duty — its 4-cell radius is a tripwire against flanks.
- Remember that paratroopers fly over fog freely: range limits apply, but darkness does not stop a drop.

## Spectators see everything

A spectator — someone watching without a seat, including you after surrendering or being eliminated — sees the **whole board**: both players' troops, pipes, and territory, with no fog at all. Spectators issue no orders; the view is strictly read-only.

## The numbers

| Value | Shipped value |
| --- | --- |
| Sensor radius | 4 cells per stack |
| Metric | Chebyshev (square radius, diagonals count as 1) |
| Memory | None — abandoned ground goes dark |
