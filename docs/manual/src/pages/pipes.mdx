# Pipes

This page explains how to lay pipes, how troop flow works through them, and why supply lines matter.

## Laying pipes

Pipes connect a cell to an adjacent cell in one of four directions: north, east, south, or west. A cell can hold **up to four directional pipes** — one per direction.

- Click near the edge of a selected cell (or press i/j/k/l) to toggle a pipe toward that edge.
- Right-click or Alt+click (or Alt+i/j/k/l) lays an **exclusive pipe**: it replaces all other pipes in that cell with a single pipe toward the chosen edge.
- Space clears all pipes in the selected cell.

See [Controls](./controls) for the full command tables.

## How flow works

Each cell has a total flow budget of **12 troops per tick**, split evenly among its outgoing pipes: `perPipe = floor(12 / numPipes)`. The slope then adjusts each pipe's share. Here are the per-pipe amounts for a well-supplied source cell (Δ = destination elevation − source elevation):

| Outgoing pipes | Per-pipe share on flat terrain | Downhill Δ=1 / Δ≥5 | Uphill Δ=1 / stall point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 12 | 13 / 17 | 11 / Δ≥12 |
| 2 | 6 | 7 / 11 | 5 / Δ≥6 |
| 3 | 4 | 5 / 9 | 3 / Δ≥4 |
| 4 | 3 | 4 / 8 | 2 / Δ≥3 |

Downhill adds up to 5 troops per pipe (`flowDownhillStep × min(|Δ|, flowSlopeDeltaCap)`). Uphill subtracts 1 per elevation step (`flowUphillStep × Δ`), down to zero. The stall point therefore depends on how many pipes leave the source cell. These are per-pipe rates; available troops, reserves, and destination capacity can limit what moves in a tick.

Flow is a **transfer, not a copy**: troops sent along a pipe are removed from the source cell as the destination receives them. A source does not keep troops that it has sent.

### The uphill trap

Uphill pipes are the classic new-player trap. You can lay them, and they appear on the board, but every step of climb costs speed. Gentle uphill pipes still move troops — just slowly. A pipe stalls at Δ≥12 with one outgoing pipe, Δ≥6 with two, Δ≥4 with three, or Δ≥3 with four. A stalled pipe renders as a **hollow triangle** on the board, so you can spot a dead supply line at a glance. When a push stalls for no apparent reason, check the source cell's outgoing pipe count as well as the elevation.

### Two more limits

- Water stops flow: a pipe pointing into water delivers nothing.
- Destination cells cap at 30 troops; a full cell accepts nothing until it has room.

## Pipe slope colors and intensity

Each pipe triangle on the board is colored by its slope classification:

| Slope | Color | Visual treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <EuropaPipeSlope direction="downhill"></EuropaPipeSlope> Downhill | Green | Filled triangle; size scales with gradient strength |
| <EuropaPipeSlope direction="flat"></EuropaPipeSlope> Flat | Amber | Filled triangle; smallest size (no gradient signal) |
| <EuropaPipeSlope direction="uphill"></EuropaPipeSlope> Uphill | Red | Filled triangle; size scales with gradient strength |
| <EuropaPipeSlope direction="stalled"></EuropaPipeSlope> Stalled | Gray | Hollow (outline-only) triangle; fixed size |

**Intensity** encodes gradient steepness as triangle size. Stronger slopes produce bigger triangles:

- **Downhill intensity**: Δ=1 produces a small triangle; Δ≥5 (the maximum bonus) produces the largest.
- **Uphill intensity**: size tracks the fraction of the per-pipe share lost to the climb (`min(flowUphillStep × Δ, perPipe) / perPipe`). The same Δ can look steeper when the source has more outgoing pipes; for example, Δ=2 reaches 2/3 intensity with four pipes. Stalled pipes use the fixed-size hollow treatment instead.
- **Flat pipes** have the smallest triangles (no gradient signal).
- At low zoom levels, a minimum triangle floor ensures pipes remain visible.

## Feeding and decay

Troops cut off from supply do not wait politely — they die:

- A **stack** (all the troops sitting in one cell) in an open cell with **no incoming pipe flow loses 1 troop per tick** to decay.
- City cells never decay: their own production feeds them every tick.
- Any friendly inflow counts as feeding, even a trickle — the minimum non-zero flow is 1 troop per tick, and it keeps a forward garrison alive.

### Mutual feeding

Two cells piping into each other sustain each other indefinitely: each counts as fed by the other's flow, so neither decays. This is the standard way to hold a stable front line — pipe your forward cells into each other and neither rots.

Reserves interact with decay too: reserved troops cannot be eaten by decay. See [Reserves](./reserves).

## Pipes as tactics

- **Arteries**: long chains of downhill/flat pipes stream fresh troops from safe cities toward the front.
- **Severing**: paratroopers clear every pipe in the cell they land on — enemy supply lines are targets (see [Special weapons](./special-weapons)).
- **Stalemates**: two mutually-fed fronts can outlast anything thrown at them; breaking one usually means cutting the other's feed with paratroopers or guns.
